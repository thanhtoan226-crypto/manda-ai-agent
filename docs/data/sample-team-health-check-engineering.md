### Strengths to Build On

- **Speedy meeting adoption is above org average (58.2% vs 55.6%)** — The team is embracing shorter meeting formats, particularly in ad-hoc sessions. Engineers in the Enterprise Apps squad lead the way at 67.3% speedy adoption. This is a cultural strength worth celebrating and spreading — when the team controls the format, they choose efficiency.

- **Strong technical collaboration culture** — 31% of meeting time is in Supporting Individuals (pairing, debugging, mentoring). This is higher than any other department and reflects a genuinely collaborative engineering culture. New hires consistently cite "access to senior engineers for live problem-solving" as a top positive in onboarding surveys.

- **Low cancellation rate on 1:1s (3.2%)** — Despite the high meeting volume, managers in Engineering almost never cancel their 1:1s. The few cancellations are all illness or leave-related. This signals that managers value and protect individual time with their reports.

- **Active external engagement drives strategic value** — The 23.1% external meeting share is driven by active vendor management (Google, Atlassian, Searce) and partnership work. This is not waste — it's strategic relationship management that directly supports the GWS migration and technology platform investments.

### Areas of Concern

- **After-hours load is concentrated and persistent** — 52.4 hours of after-hours meetings in May, affecting 18 of 47 engineers. The Searce offshore coordination accounts for 38% of this time. Engineers Mart Thompson, Jessie Chen, and Damien Kowalski each logged 4+ after-hours meetings. Three consecutive months above 45 hours team-wide is a burnout signal that warrants intervention.

- **1-on-1 coverage gap affects 13 engineers** — 13 team members do not have a regular recurring 1:1 with their manager. Most are senior ICs whose managers assume they "don't need" regular check-ins, but the data shows these engineers have higher after-hours meeting time and lower speedy meeting adoption — potential early disengagement signals.

- **Large meeting percentage (33.6%) is dragging down quality** — The ETech Wednesday Update (42 people), Due Diligence Standup (33-35 people), and Q3 Planning Kickoff (22 people) are the worst offenders. These three meetings alone account for $11,400/month and have an average quality score of 51.2%. The larger the meeting, the lower the quality — this is a structural problem, not a people problem.

- **Thursday is a team-wide bottleneck** — 29 of 47 engineers have their heaviest meeting day on Thursday, with an average of 5.1 hours of meetings. 14 engineers have 3+ back-to-back blocks on Thursdays. The clustering creates a "meeting marathon" effect that depletes focus time for the entire team mid-week.

**Members missing regular 1:1s (13 engineers):**

8 senior engineers and 5 mid-level engineers do not have a recurring 1:1 with their manager. In most cases, the manager has commented that these are "experienced self-starters who don't need regular check-ins." However, the data tells a different story:

- These 13 engineers have 28% higher after-hours meeting time than the covered group
- Their speedy meeting adoption is 12pp lower (49.1% vs 61.3%)
- 4 of the 13 have had no 1:1 with anyone in their management chain in the past 60 days

**Recommendation:** Institute a minimum fortnightly 1:1 for all team members regardless of level. The "they don't need it" assumption is contradicted by the engagement data.

### Collaboration Patterns

**Internal vs External Split:**
- Internal meetings: 76.9% of meeting time
- External meetings: 23.1% of meeting time (highest in the company)

**Cross-team Collaboration:**
- Engineering ↔ Product: 14.2% of meeting time (healthy)
- Engineering ↔ Data & Analytics: 8.6% (growing, up from 5.1% in March)
- Engineering ↔ Design: 4.3% (below ideal, Design reports wanting more Eng input)
- Engineering ↔ Operations: 3.8% (appropriate)

**Meeting Clustering:**
- Peak day: Thursday (5.1 hrs avg per engineer)
- Quietest day: Friday (1.4 hrs avg)
- Peak hour: 10:00 AM (18.4% of all meeting time starts at 10am)
- "No meeting" blocks: Only 6 engineers have a protected focus block on their calendar

**Isolation Signals:**
- 3 engineers (Lin Wei, Taylor Brooks, Sam Patel) had fewer than 2 cross-team meetings in May
- 2 engineers had zero meetings they organised — their calendars are entirely shaped by others
- These engineers are all in the "missing 1:1" group, compounding the disengagement risk

### Discussion Starters for Team Lead

- "Our after-hours meeting load has been above 45 hours for three months running, mostly driven by Searce coordination. What would it take to establish a fixed overlap window and batch these into fewer, more predictable slots?"

- "Thirteen of our engineers don't have regular 1:1s. I know the assumption is that senior people don't need them, but the data shows they're actually the ones with more after-hours meetings and lower meeting quality. Could we pilot a fortnightly check-in for everyone?"

- "Our large meeting percentage (33.6%) is way above the org median (26.4%), and it's dragging our quality score down. The ETech Wednesday Update with 42 people and the Due Diligence Standup with 35 — are these still serving their purpose for everyone in the room?"

- "Thursday is becoming a team-wide meeting marathon — 29 of 47 engineers have their heaviest day then. Would it help to declare Thursday morning as a 'no large forum' block, or redistribute some of the standing meetings to Monday?"

- "Our junior engineers (Lin, Alex, Sam) have great focus time but almost no cross-team exposure. They're not in the big alignment forums, and they're not getting pulled into ad-hoc collaboration. How can we create more structured inclusion for them?"

- "Jessie Chen is carrying a heavy load — 41.2 hours, 11 back-to-back days, and the most after-hours time on the team. As a tech lead, she's expected to be accessible, but this pace isn't sustainable. What support can we give her?"

- "Design tells us they want more Engineering input in their process, but we only spend 4.3% of our meeting time with them. Are we accidentally creating a silo between Eng and Design that will slow us down later?"
