### Key Signals

- **Meeting cost crossed $680K in a single week** for the first time, driven by a spike in cross-departmental planning sessions ahead of Q3 kickoff. The 6.7% week-over-week increase is the largest single-week jump since January's all-hands surge.

- **After-hours meetings surged 23%** to 38.4 hours company-wide. The primary driver is Searce offshore coordination (12.6 hrs) and a Google Workspace Discovery sprint (8.2 hrs). Engineering and Product teams account for 72% of after-hours time.

- **Speedy meeting adoption reached a new high (63.1%)**, continuing its steady climb from 54% in January. The 25- and 50-minute format is now the default for most ad-hoc meetings across the organisation.

- **1-on-1 coverage held steady at 73.9%** despite the overall meeting volume increase. This suggests managers are protecting their 1:1 time even as other meetings crowd their calendars — a positive cultural signal.

### Trends Worth Noting

- **Quality score declined for the third consecutive week** (70.2% → 69.1% → 68.1% → 66.8%). The decline correlates with rising meeting volume — as more meetings are added, agenda usage drops (58.7% → 56.4%) and context clarity suffers. This is a leading indicator of meeting quality degradation if the volume trend continues.

- **Large meeting percentage is creeping up** (27.8% → 29.1%). Five new recurring forums with 10+ attendees were created this week, including a "Q3 Planning Kick-off" (22 people), "Product-Eng Alignment" (15 people), and "Data Platform Roadmap" (18 people). Without intervention, these will compound weekly costs.

- **External meeting share is rising** (21.4% → 22.6%), driven by active vendor negotiations with Google, Atlassian, and Salesforce. The external cluster is concentrated in Engineering and Procurement — 14 people account for 68% of all external meeting time.

### Red Flags & Recommendations

- **Engineering quality score (64.2%) is 2.6pp below company average** and has declined 4.1pp over the past month. The high large-meeting percentage (33.6%) and low agenda usage (43.2%) are correlated. **Recommendation:** Require agendas for all recurring meetings with 8+ attendees in Engineering. Pilot with the ETech Wednesday Update (42 people, currently no agenda).

- **Five new large forums created this week without sunset clauses.** The Q3 Planning Kick-off (22 people), Product-Eng Alignment (15 people), and Data Platform Roadmap (18 people) were all added as recurring weekly meetings. At current rates, these will add ~$34,000/month in meeting cost. **Recommendation:** All new recurring meetings with 10+ attendees should have a defined end date (maximum 8 weeks) and a designated owner responsible for agenda quality.

- **Searce offshore coordination is driving after-hours load.** 12.6 hours of after-hours time this week came from Searce-related meetings, affecting 9 engineers. **Recommendation:** Establish a fixed overlap window (e.g., 4-6pm AEST) and batch coordination into 2-3 weekly slots rather than ad-hoc evening calls.

- **Data & Analytics has the lowest 1-on-1 coverage (69.8%)** and highest recurring meeting ratio (71%). Team members are spending disproportionate time in group forums at the expense of individual manager check-ins. **Recommendation:** Audit the 6 recurring Data Platform meetings and consolidate where possible; redirect saved time to 1:1 coverage.

- **Design is the only department improving quality week-over-week** (71.3% → 72.1%), driven by strong agenda usage (78.4%) and low large-meeting percentage (18.4%). Their practice of "agenda-as-invite" (including the agenda in the calendar invite itself) could be shared as an org-wide best practice.
