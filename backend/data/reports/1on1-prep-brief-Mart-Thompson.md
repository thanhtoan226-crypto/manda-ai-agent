### Strengths to Acknowledge

- **Strong speedy meeting adoption (42.8%)** - Mart defaults to 25- and 50-minute slots more often than most senior engineers (peer median 31.5%). This keeps his meetings tight and intentional. His 1:1 technical pairing sessions are consistently 25 minutes, which keeps them focused.

- **Low large meeting exposure (18.1%)** - Mart avoids big forums. Only 13 of his 72 meetings had 8+ attendees, compared to a 24.7% peer median. He's getting information through smaller, more effective channels rather than broadcast-style meetings.

- **Good response rate (94.1%)** - Mart responds to nearly all invitations promptly. While not at Chris's 99.2%, this is well above the 82.6% peer median and signals calendar discipline.

- **Active in Supporting Individuals category (32.6%)** - Over a third of Mart's meeting time is spent helping others — technical pairing, mentoring junior engineers, and cross-team problem-solving. This is a strong signal of collaboration and knowledge-sharing, not just "being in meetings."

### Patterns Worth Discussing

1. **Outside-hours meetings are elevated (3.2 hrs across 4 meetings in May)**

   Mart had 4 meetings outside standard hours: two late-evener syncs with the Searce offshore team (Tue 7pm, Thu 6:30pm), one early-morning Google Workspace demo (Mon 7:15am), and one Saturday ad-hoc incident review (30 min). The peer median is 0.8 hrs/month. This is the third consecutive month above 2 hrs.

   You might ask: "I've noticed you've been taking more after-hours meetings over the last few months, especially with the Searce team. Are these essential for your role, or could we shift some of them to overlap better with your hours?"

2. **Wednesday and Thursday are back-to-back heavy (73% of weekly meeting time compressed into 2 days)**

   His day-of-week distribution: Mon 4.2 hrs, Tue 5.8 hrs, Wed 9.1 hrs, Thu 10.4 hrs, Fri 1.3 hrs. Thursday is particularly dense — 6 of his 7 back-to-back blocks (3+ meetings in a row) fell on Thursdays. Friday is almost meeting-free, which is great for focus time but creates a compressed mid-week crunch.

   You might ask: "Your Thursdays are really packed — six back-to-back blocks last month. Would it help to redistribute some of those meetings to Monday or Friday to create more breathing room?"

3. **Meeting hours have been trending upward over the past 3 months**

   The 4-month trend: Feb 26.1 hrs, Mar 29.4 hrs, Apr 31.7 hrs, May 34.8 hrs. Each month has added roughly 3 hours. The growth is driven by more Supporting Individuals and Alignment meetings, particularly cross-team coordination around the GWS Technical Discovery project and ramped-up Searce engagement.

   You might ask: "Your meeting load has been climbing steadily — about 3 more hours each month since February. Do you feel this is temporary due to the Google discovery work, or is it becoming the new normal?"

4. **Low meeting organisation rate (22%) suggests Mart is mostly responding, not driving**

   Mart organises only 16 of his 72 meetings. Most of his calendar is shaped by others — Chris's team standups, vendor syncs, and cross-team alignment forums. This isn't necessarily bad for an IC, but the low organisation rate combined with rising meeting hours could mean he's being pulled into things he doesn't need to attend.

   You might ask: "You're in a lot of meetings that others organise — only 22% are ones you set up. Are there any recurring meetings on your calendar where your attendance might be optional, or where a written update could replace your presence?"

### Meetings He Organises (16 meetings, 22% of his total)

Mart organises a modest portion of his meetings. Key ones he runs:

- Technical pairing sessions with junior engineers (2-3 per week, 25 min each)
- GWS Technical Discovery Sync (weekly, 8 people) — took over from Chris in late April
- Ad-hoc incident response syncs (3 in May, 15-25 min each)
- 1:1 preparation sessions before his weekly with Chris

His organised meetings have a quality score of 74.3%, significantly higher than the meetings others organise that he attends (61.8%). Agenda usage for his meetings is 56.3% vs 21.4% for those he merely attends.

**LLM Feedback:** Mart's self-organised meetings are notably higher quality (74.3% vs 61.8%) than the ones he's invited to. He writes agendas for over half his own meetings but rarely receives agendas from others. This suggests Mart values meeting structure when he's in control but tolerates low-quality meetings from others. Recommendation: Mart could advocate for agenda standards in the recurring forums he attends — his own practice shows he benefits from them. The GWS Technical Discovery Sync he now runs is an opportunity to model this for the team.

## Context and Trends

**Trend Direction:** Mart's meeting load has been steadily increasing — 26.1 hrs (Feb) → 29.4 hrs (Mar) → 31.7 hrs (Apr) → 34.8 hrs (May). The growth is consistent at ~3 hrs/month, driven by more cross-team coordination and his expanding role in the GWS Technical Discovery project. He's still below the Engineering Manager median (62.5 hrs) but well above the Senior Engineer median (28.2 hrs).

**Wellness:** Some concern. 3.2 hours outside work hours in May, his third consecutive month above 2 hrs. The Searce offshore coordination is the primary driver. While not alarming, it's worth monitoring — senior engineers with sustained after-hours load are at higher burnout risk.

**As a Team Contributor:** Mart is clearly a high-value technical resource. His Supporting Individuals time (32.6%) is above typical for a senior engineer and reflects active mentoring, pairing, and cross-team help. This is a strength, but it also means his calendar is shaped by others' needs more than his own priorities.

**External Engagement Context:** The Google and Searce vendor engagement mirrors Chris's pattern but at a more technical level — Mart is in the implementation details rather than the strategic relationship management. This is appropriate for his level.

**Notable:** Mart's low meeting organisation rate (22%) combined with his high impact in the meetings he does organise (74.3% quality score) suggests he's ready for more meeting leadership — setting agendas, driving outcomes, and potentially running more forums. He just needs the mandate and encouragement to do so.
